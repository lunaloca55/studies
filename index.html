Section 1: The Graduate Enrollment Funnel

Overview

The graduate enrollment funnel represents the journey from first awareness to matriculation. Understanding each stage and the conversion points between them is essential for managing enrollment effectively.

The Seven Stages

Stage Definition Key Actions
1. Awareness Potential students become aware of your programs Advertising, SEO, events, brand building
2. Interest Prospects submit RFI or request information Landing pages, email nurture, webinars
3. Consideration Active evaluation and comparison of options Counselor outreach, campus visits, testimonials
4. Application Prospects start and submit applications Application support, reminders, technical help
5. Decision Review, admit, and present offers Timely decisions, financial aid, yield campaigns
6. Enrollment Admits accept offers and complete enrollment Deposits, registration, orientation, melt prevention
7. Post-Enrollment Students begin program and persist to completion Retention support, advising, engagement

Sample Funnel Flow

Typical Graduate Program Example:
  • → 10,000 Prospects (website visitors, ad impressions)
  • → 1,000 Inquiries (10% conversion - RFI submissions)
  • → 150 Applicants (15% conversion - started applications)
  • → 90 Completed Apps (60% completion rate)
  • → 60 Admits (67% admit rate)
  • → 40 Deposits (67% yield rate)
  • → 38 Enrolled (95% matriculation rate)

Overall Win Rate: 3.8% (38 enrolled ÷ 1,000 inquiries)

Critical Conversion Points

Six key conversion points determine your enrollment success:

Conversion Point Typical Rate What Drives It
Prospect → Inquiry 8-12% Brand strength, targeting quality, value proposition clarity
Inquiry → Application 13-18% Nurture effectiveness, counselor outreach, program fit
Started → Completed App 50-60% Application simplicity, support availability, urgency
Completed → Admit 60-70% Program selectivity, applicant quality, standards
Admit → Deposit 60-70% Financial aid competitiveness, yield campaigns, fit
Deposit → Enrolled 95%+ Communication frequency, melt prevention, engagement

Why This Matters

Small improvements = Big results

Example: Improving inquiry→application from 15% to 17% (just 2 percentage points):

  • 1,000 inquiries × 15% = 150 applicants (baseline)
  • 1,000 inquiries × 17% = 170 applicants (improved)
  • 20 additional applicants × 3.8% overall win rate = ~1 additional enrolled student

Across 10 programs, that's 10 more students = significant revenue impact.

Section 2: Cross-Funnel KPIs

These six metrics span multiple funnel stages and provide the most comprehensive view of enrollment performance.

KPI #1: Funnel Conversion Rate

Definition

The percentage of prospects who advance from one funnel stage to the next.

Formula: (Number Advanced to Next Stage ÷ Number in Current Stage) × 100

Key Conversion Metrics to Track:

  • Inquiry-to-Application Rate: (Applicants ÷ Inquiries) × 100
  • Application Completion Rate: (Completed ÷ Started) × 100
  • Offer-to-Deposit Yield: (Deposits ÷ Admits) × 100
  • Overall Win Rate: (Enrolled ÷ Applicants) × 100
Benchmarks:
  • Inquiry → Application: 13-18%
  • Application Completion: 50-60%
  • Offer → Deposit: 60-70%
  • Overall Win Rate: 3-4%

Daily Use Cases:

  1. Weekly Monitoring: Check each Monday to spot trends before they become problems
  2. Program Comparison: Identify which programs are over/underperforming
  3. Goal Setting: Establish realistic improvement targets for each stage
  4. Forecasting: Predict enrollment based on current pipeline and historical rates
Example: You have 500 admits and need 100 enrolled students. Historical yield shows 65% deposit and 95% matriculate.
  • Expected deposits: 500 × 0.65 = 325
  • Expected enrolled: 325 × 0.95 = 309
  • Result: You'll exceed your goal by 209 students

KPI #2: Days Between Stages (Pipeline Velocity)

Definition

The average number of days it takes for a prospect to move from one stage to the next.

Formula: Average (Date Entered Next Stage - Date Entered Current Stage)

Critical Timeframes to Track:

  • Inquiry → Application: How long it takes interested prospects to apply
  • Application Started → Completed: How long applications sit incomplete
  • Application Completed → Decision: Your decision turnaround time
  • Admit → Deposit: How long admits take to commit
Benchmarks:
  • Inquiry → Application: ≤ 30 days
  • Application → Decision: < 14 days
  • Admit → Deposit: Varies by deadline structure

Goal: Optimized decline over time (faster movement = better)

Daily Use Cases:

  1. Identify Stalled Prospects: Pull list of inquiries 30+ days old who haven't applied for targeted outreach
  2. Optimize Nurture Timing: Set email campaign intervals based on average conversion time
  3. Resource Planning: Predict when application volume will hit based on inquiry timing
  4. Bottleneck Detection: If velocity suddenly slows, investigate the cause immediately
Example: Your inquiry→application velocity is 45 days (15 days over target). You have 200 inquiries from the past 30 days.
  • Action: Launch immediate re-engagement campaign to these 200 prospects
  • Include: Deadline reminder, application tips, counselor office hours
  • Impact: Even 10% response = 20 additional applications

KPI #3: Cost per Stage Transition

Definition

The total marketing and recruitment expenditure required to move one student from one stage to the next.

Formula: Total Stage Marketing Spend ÷ Number of Stage Transitions

Key Cost Metrics:

  • Cost per Lead (CPL): Marketing spend ÷ Number of inquiries
  • Cost per Applicant: Total recruitment spend ÷ Number of applicants
  • Cost per Enrolled Student (CPE): Total spend ÷ Number enrolled
Industry Benchmarks (Graduate Programs):
  • Cost per Inquiry: $140 average (varies widely by channel)
  • Cost per Enrolled Student: $2,850 average

Goal: Downward trend over time (improved efficiency)

Daily Use Cases:

  1. Channel Budget Allocation: Shift spend from high-cost to low-cost channels
  2. Campaign ROI Calculation: Determine if a campaign was worth the investment
  3. Leadership Reporting: Prove marketing efficiency and justify budget requests
  4. Program Profitability: Compare cost-to-recruit across programs
Example: You're deciding between two marketing channels:
Channel Spend Inquiries CPL Applications Cost/App
Google Ads $10,000 100 $100 17 $588
Facebook Ads $10,000 80 $125 7 $1,429

Decision: Shift budget to Google Ads (59% lower cost per applicant)

KPI #4: LTV:CAC Ratio

Definition

The ratio of Lifetime Value (total revenue from a student) to Customer Acquisition Cost (total cost to recruit and enroll them).

LTV:CAC Ratio = Lifetime Value ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost

Components:

Lifetime Value (LTV):

  • Total tuition paid over entire program
  • Fees and other revenue
  • Additional programs (certificates, second degrees)

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):

  • Marketing and advertising spend
  • Recruitment staff salaries (proportional)
  • Events, travel, materials
  • Technology costs
  • Yield activities
Target Ratios:
  • Minimum: 3:1 (earn $3 for every $1 spent)
  • Healthy: 5:1
  • Excellent: 7:1 or higher

Warning: Below 3:1 indicates unsustainable recruitment costs

Daily Use Cases:

  1. Program Investment Decisions: Which programs warrant increased marketing spend?
  2. Leadership Justification: Prove marketing ROI with clear revenue data
  3. Portfolio Management: Compare profitability across entire program portfolio
  4. Strategic Planning: Identify programs for growth vs. maintenance
Example: MBA Program
  • Tuition: $60,000 total program
  • Recruitment Cost: $3,500 per student
  • LTV:CAC = $60,000 ÷ $3,500 = 17:1 ratio
  • Interpretation: Highly profitable; consider increasing marketing investment
Example: MPA Program
  • Tuition: $35,000 total program
  • Recruitment Cost: $4,200 per student
  • LTV:CAC = $35,000 ÷ $4,200 = 8.3:1 ratio
  • Interpretation: Healthy but room for efficiency gains

KPI #5: Lead Quality by Channel

Definition

A measure of how effectively each marketing channel generates leads that convert deeper into the funnel and ultimately enroll.

Quality Score = (Conversion Rate × Volume) ÷ Cost

Metrics to Track by Channel:

  • Inquiry Volume: Raw number of leads generated
  • Inquiry-to-Application Rate: What % actually apply?
  • Application-to-Enrollment Rate: What % enroll?
  • Cost per Inquiry: Spend ÷ Inquiries
  • Cost per Enrolled Student: Total cost to final enrollment

Common Channels to Compare:

Channel Typical Use Quality Indicator
Organic Search High-intent prospects Usually highest conversion rates
Paid Search Targeted keywords High quality if targeting is right
Social Media (Paid) Awareness and retargeting Variable; test continuously
Email Campaigns Nurture and conversion High for engaged lists
Events/Fairs In-person connection Lower volume, higher quality
Referrals Alumni/faculty recommendations Typically highest quality

Daily Use Cases:

  1. Budget Reallocation: Shift spend from low to high-performing channels weekly
  2. Targeting Optimization: Refine underperforming channels rather than abandon them
  3. Forecasting: Predict downstream results based on channel mix
  4. Testing: Run A/B tests on messaging by channel
Example Analysis:
Channel Inquiries Applied Conv % Enrolled Decision
Organic Search 200 45 22.5% 8 ✓ Maintain investment
Facebook 500 35 7% 4 ⚠ Optimize or reduce
Referrals 50 18 36% 5 ✓ Encourage more

Action: Invest in referral program, optimize Facebook targeting, maintain organic SEO

KPI #6: Source-to-Enrollment Attribution

Definition

Full-funnel tracking that connects each enrolled student back to their original marketing source and tracks all touchpoints along their journey.

UTM Parameters to Track:

  • utm_source: Where traffic came from (google, facebook, email)
  • utm_medium: How they arrived (cpc, social, email, referral)
  • utm_campaign: Specific campaign name
  • utm_content: Specific ad or content piece
  • utm_term: Keyword (for paid search)

Attribution Models:

Model Credit Assignment Best For
First-Touch 100% credit to first interaction Understanding awareness channels
Last-Touch 100% credit to last interaction before conversion Understanding conversion drivers
Multi-Touch Equal credit across all touchpoints Comprehensive journey view
Time-Decay More credit to recent interactions Balanced view with recency bias

Daily Use Cases:

  1. Complete Journey Mapping: Understand full path from awareness to enrollment
  2. Early Funnel Investment: Justify spend on awareness activities that don't directly convert
  3. Channel Interaction: See how channels work together (e.g., Google → Email → Event)
  4. Strategic Optimization: Optimize entire recruitment strategy, not just last touch
Example Journey:
  1. Day 1: Student clicks Facebook ad → Views program page
  2. Day 7: Returns via Google search → Downloads brochure
  3. Day 14: Receives email → Registers for webinar
  4. Day 21: Attends webinar → Submits RFI
  5. Day 45: Email nurture sequence → Starts application
  6. Day 52: Counselor call → Completes application
  7. Day 80: Admitted → Accepts offer

Attribution: Facebook initiated, Email nurtured, Webinar converted

Without full attribution, you might only credit the counselor call, missing the importance of Facebook and webinars.

Essential Calculation Formulas

Conversion Rates

Conversion Rate = (Number Who Advanced / Number in Starting Stage) × 100

Cost Metrics

Cost per Lead = Total Marketing Spend / Number of Leads

Cost per Applicant = Total Recruitment Spend / Number of Applicants

Cost per Enrolled = Total Recruitment & Marketing Spend / Number Enrolled

Yield Metrics

Overall Yield Rate = (Number Enrolled / Number Admitted) × 100

Offer-to-Deposit Yield = (Number Deposited / Number Admitted) × 100

Deposit-to-Enrollment = (Number Enrolled / Number Deposited) × 100

ROI Metrics

LTV:CAC Ratio = Lifetime Value / Customer Acquisition Cost

Lifetime Value = Average Tuition per Term × Average Terms Enrolled

Marketing ROI = (Revenue - Marketing Spend) / Marketing Spend × 100

Retention Metrics

Retention Rate = (Students Returning / Initial Cohort) × 100

Attrition Rate = (Students Who Left / Initial Cohort) × 100

Note: Retention Rate + Attrition Rate = 100%

Email Metrics

Open Rate = (Opens / Delivered Emails) × 100

Click Rate = (Clicks / Delivered Emails) × 100

Click-to-Open Rate = (Clicks / Opens) × 100

Section 4: Core Technology Tools

Salesforce (CRM)

Purpose

Customer Relationship Management platform for managing student data, tracking interactions, and managing the recruitment pipeline.

Key Objects:

  • Contact: Individual person record (student, prospect, applicant)
  • Lead: Early-stage prospect not yet qualified
  • Opportunity: Tracks application pipeline from prospect to matriculation
  • Campaign: Plans, tracks, and analyzes marketing efforts

Opportunity Stages:

  1. Prospective - Initial interest
  2. In-Progress Application - Started but not submitted
  3. Application - Complete application submitted
  4. Admit - Offered admission
  5. Deposit - Accepted offer and deposited
  6. Enrolled - Matriculated

Daily Use:

  • Track all prospect and student interactions
  • Generate reports on funnel performance
  • Manage recruitment campaigns
  • Forecast enrollment based on pipeline

Marketing Cloud

Purpose

Digital marketing platform for creating, automating, and analyzing email campaigns and multi-channel journeys.

Key Components:

  • Email Studio: Create and send email campaigns
  • Journey Builder: Automated, personalized communication sequences
  • Audience Builder: Segment and target specific groups
  • Content Builder: Manage email templates and content
  • Automation Studio: Automate repetitive tasks

Integration with Salesforce:

  • Data syncs every 15 minutes
  • Salesforce data available for targeting and personalization
  • Email engagement tracked back to Salesforce (IERs)

Daily Use:

  • Send targeted email campaigns
  • Create automated nurture journeys
  • Track email engagement metrics
  • Segment audiences for personalization

Campaign vs. Journey: Key Differences

Feature Campaign Journey
Purpose Track and manage marketing initiatives Automate personalized communication sequences
Scope Broader; overall strategy Granular; individual experience
Automation May include automated elements Highly automated; behavior-triggered
Use Case Open house promotional push Incomplete application nurture sequence

Google Analytics

Purpose

Web analytics platform to track website traffic, user behavior, and campaign effectiveness.

Key Metrics to Track:

  • Sessions & Users: Website traffic volume
  • Traffic Sources: Where visitors come from
  • Behavior Flow: Path through website
  • Goal Completions: RFI submissions, application starts
  • Campaign Performance: UTM tracking for all marketing

Daily Use:

  • Monitor website traffic trends
  • Evaluate campaign effectiveness
  • Identify popular content
  • Track goal conversions

Essential Reports & Dashboards

Weekly Funnel Report

Track these metrics every Monday:

  • Count at each funnel stage
  • Week-over-week change
  • Conversion rates between stages
  • Days between stages
  • Alerts for significant drops

Monthly Marketing Performance Report

  • Lead volume by channel
  • Cost per lead by channel
  • Conversion rate by channel
  • Cost per enrolled by channel
  • LTV:CAC ratio by program

Quarterly Strategic Review

  • Year-over-year comparisons
  • Trend analysis
  • Benchmark comparisons
  • Program-level deep dives
  • Strategic recommendations

Section 6: Quick Start Guide

Your First 30 Days with Metrics

Week 1: Establish Baseline

Goal: Know where you stand today
  1. Pull current numbers for all 6 cross-funnel KPIs
  2. Document where each metric comes from (data source)
  3. Create simple dashboard or spreadsheet
  4. Share baseline with your team
Deliverable: Baseline metrics dashboard

Week 2: Set Targets

Goal: Define success
  1. Compare your baseline to benchmarks in this guide
  2. Set realistic 3-month improvement goals
  3. Assign ownership: Who's responsible for each KPI?
  4. Schedule weekly 30-minute metric review meeting
Deliverable: Metric targets and accountability chart

Week 3: Identify Quick Wins

Goal: Get early momentum
  1. Find your biggest gap vs. benchmark
  2. Pick ONE tactical improvement to implement
  3. Launch the improvement and track impact
  4. Document what you learn
Example: If application completion is 40% (vs. 55% target), send automated reminders to incomplete applications

Week 4: Build Cadence

Goal: Make metrics routine
  1. Confirm weekly metric review is happening
  2. Create simple decision framework: "If X drops below Y, we do Z"
  3. Train team on how to use metrics for daily decisions
  4. Plan Month 2 improvements based on Week 3 learnings
Deliverable: Sustainable metric review process

Monday Morning Ritual

Start each week with this 15-minute check-in:

  1. 5 minutes: Review funnel counts at each stage
  2. 5 minutes: Check week-over-week conversion rate changes
  3. 5 minutes: Identify and assign any concerning trends
Sample Monday Check:
  • ✓ Inquiries: 47 this week (up from 42 last week - good trend)
  • ⚠ Inquiry→App conversion: Dropped from 15% to 11% - ACTION: Launch re-engagement campaign
  • ✓ Application completion: Steady at 58%
  • ✓ Offers made: 12 (on track for monthly goal of 50)

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ Don't:
  • Track too many metrics at once (start with 3-5)
  • Compare yourself only to external benchmarks (your trend matters more)
  • React to single data points (look for trends over 3-4 weeks)
  • Set unachievable targets (2-5% improvement is excellent)
  • Forget to celebrate wins (acknowledge improvements publicly)
✅ Do:
  • Start simple and add complexity over time
  • Focus on what you can control and improve
  • Use metrics to inform decisions, not just report
  • Share data transparently with your team
  • Iterate based on what you learn

Small improvements compound over time into significant results.
